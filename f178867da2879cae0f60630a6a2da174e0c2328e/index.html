Layer 01

Foundational Stack

Resolve directionally before generating interventions, because they determine which interventions are coherent.

Operating Model

Org structure, headcount philosophy (lean core vs. full stack), decision rights.

Business Model

Asset-light vs. heavy. Majority of activities done in-house versus externally. Build-to-sell vs. lease vs. operate.

Capital Structure

Equity mix (promoters, funds, retail). Debt strategy. Project SPVs vs. consolidated.

+ Data & Agentic Architecture (overarching)

Cuts across all three choices above. Single source of truth. Real-time vs. batch. API-first design. Data ownership model.

Agentic AI architecture — which processes are candidates for autonomous AI agents, and what guardrails govern them.

Also relevant but secondary: Brand architecture (language, positioning), ecosystem design (partnership models, vertical integration posture), and culture & values (innovation focus, speed vs. deliberation, transparency, customer obsession, risk tolerance).
Layer 02

Generative Canvas

Four vectors you combine across the value chain. Each combination is a prompt: "At this step, apply this treatment, using this capability, to unlock this benefit."

V1

Process — "Where in the value chain?"

The lifecycle tree from land to living. Defined separately — not repeated here.

V2

Treatment — "What's my move?"

8 moves available at any process step. Ordered most disruptive → least.

Flow moves — change whether, when, or how steps connect
What if this step didn't exist at all? Not cost-cutting — the question is whether the function this step serves can be made unnecessary by a change elsewhere.
What if this happened at a fundamentally different time — earlier, later, or in parallel? Includes parallelization (the most common compression lever) and trigger redesign (event-driven vs. calendar-driven).
What step is missing that would prevent downstream failure or unlock new value? The contrarian move — sometimes the highest-leverage intervention is adding a step that doesn't exist.
Merge: Can two steps with a handoff between them become one step with one owner? Every handoff is a delay, an information loss, and an accountability gap. Fragment: Conversely, should one monolithic step be broken into smaller, iterative micro-steps? Large-phase launches → micro-launches with feedback loops. Waterfall design → iterative design sprints.
Execution moves — change how the step is performed
Can the human be removed entirely? Full replacement — the step still happens, no one does it. Distinct from Augment: there is no human in the loop.
Can the human stay but make a materially better or faster decision with a machine co-pilot? The human retains judgment; the machine expands their capacity.
Can this be converted from bespoke-every-time to a repeatable template, playbook, or configurable module? Every bespoke instance reintroduces variability, cost, time, and error.
Value move — change what value the step creates
Beyond its primary function, what additional value could this step generate? Four sub-types: delight layer (customer experience), data capture layer (every step feeds intelligence), feedback loop (output feeds back to other stages), brand signal (the process itself communicates quality/trust).
Example — applied to "Design Brief" stage

Scanning the 8 moves against the design brief: Could we Eliminate the standalone brief entirely if the feasibility model auto-configures a design template? Resequence it to run in parallel with feasibility instead of after? Merge the brief and TDD alignment into one cross-functional workshop, or Fragment it into iterative sprints? Automate the first draft from land parameters + demand data? Standardize it into templates by product type (70% pre-filled)? Elevate it by capturing the assumptions behind every decision as a knowledge asset for future projects?

Most real recommendations combine only 2–3 moves.

V3

Capability — "What type of skill am I deploying?"

8 functional modes, ordered as an intelligence stack from raw signal to institutional wisdom. Not technologies — what the capability does for you.

"What's actually happening in the physical world?" — Acquiring data from reality that was previously invisible or delayed. Drones, IoT, satellite, GIS, behavioral tracking. Without capture, everything downstream runs on assumptions.
"How do I connect what's currently siloed?" — Unifying data, workflows, and actors across boundaries. BIM as single source of truth. API-connected stacks. Unified customer records. The connective tissue that makes every other mode possible.
"What patterns or anomalies exist in data I already have?" — Finding signal in noise. Backward-looking: what IS and what WAS. Detecting cost anomalies, identifying which specs correlate with higher NPS, surfacing which assumptions were wrong.
"What is likely to happen next, under what scenarios?" — Forward-looking. Demand forecasting, delay probability, cost overrun risk, scenario simulation. Where analysis becomes foresight.
"Can I create a new artifact from parameters and constraints?" — Most transformative for knowledge-heavy steps. Auto-generated designs, briefs, contracts, marketing content. Not search-and-retrieve — creating something new.
"Given constraints, what is the best configuration?" — Finding the mathematical or logical optimum. FSI maximization, dynamic pricing, schedule optimization, unit mix optimization. Not patterns (Analyze) or forecasts (Predict) — the best answer.
"How do I coordinate multiple actors toward a shared outcome?" Platform-based coordination, multi-project resource allocation, ecosystem partner management. Makes the "operating system" model possible.
"How do I get systematically better at this over time?" Every other mode operates on a snapshot; Learn accumulates. Post-project reviews, evolving cost benchmarks, design libraries updated from resident feedback, ML models that retrain.
Example — applied to "Design Brief" stage

Capture site data via drone/GIS instead of relying on memory from a visit. Integrate CRM + competitive intel + financial model into one brief workspace. Analyze the last 20 projects to see which unit-mix assumptions were right vs. wrong. Predict demand by unit type for the next 24 months. Generate a first-draft brief from land parameters + micro-market profile. Optimize unit mix to maximize IRR given demand and FSI constraints. Orchestrate inputs from sales, finance, design, and construction into one workflow. Learn by comparing every brief's assumptions against actuals post-completion, feeding deviations into the next brief.

V4

Benefit — "What am I solving for?"

Two-level tree. Outcomes are what the board measures. Drivers are the intermediate operational improvements that cause outcomes. Same driver can serve multiple outcomes. Same outcome served by multiple drivers.

Level 1 — Outcomes (the "so what")
Faster time-to-market, faster cycles, faster response. Land-to-launch, launch-to-sellout, booking-to-possession.
Lower cost per sqft delivered. Not just construction — includes cost of capital (faster cycles), cost of sales (better channels), cost of quality failure (fewer defects).
Fewer defects, higher consistency, brand-standard adherence. Measured in defect rates, snag counts, rework costs, customer satisfaction, referral rates.
Customer experience across the full journey. Distinct from Quality: a defect-free apartment with 12 months of silence post-booking is bad CX.
More projects, more cities, more product types, more revenue per customer. Ability to enter new revenue pools (services, data, management fees).
Ability to identify, mitigate, and absorb risks — demand slowdown, cost spikes, regulatory surprises, contractor failures, concentration exposure. Proactive risk management, not just resilience after the fact.
Level 2 — Drivers (the operational "how")
Real-time, transparent information — internal (teams see what's happening now, not last month) and external (customers/investors see it too). Replaces reporting culture with sensing culture. → Speed, Quality, Experience
Ability to see what's coming and act before it arrives. Prediction + preparedness. Replaces reactive firefighting with proactive intervention. → Cost, Risk Management, Speed
Consistency, repeatability, portability. The most powerful driver for a multi-project developer. Standard design libraries, spec books, processes. Enables geographic expansion without reinventing the model. → Cost, Quality, Growth
Personalization without proportional complexity. Only works on top of Standardization (customize the surface, standardize the structure). Not the opposite of D3 — a layer above it. → Experience, Growth
Ability to adapt, iterate, pivot. Micro-phased launches, modular tech stacks, flexible partnerships. The opposite is rigidity — locked into a plan with no ability to course-correct. → Speed, Risk Management, Growth
Connected systems, people, and data. The plumbing. BIM → procurement → site → finance. Eliminates re-entry, handoff delays, and information asymmetry. Not glamorous, but its absence is the root cause of most inefficiency. → Speed, Cost, Visibility
Example — applied to "Design Brief" stage

The brief stage can be optimized for Speed (done in 5 days not 6 weeks via templates + data-fed inputs) and Cost (right unit mix from the start = less unsold inventory). The key drivers: Standardization (template briefs per product type), Visibility (all stakeholders see the same real-time dashboard of assumptions and sign-off status), and Foresight (demand prediction and scenario modeling built in, not done as a separate exercise later).

How the Vectors Combine
At [V1: Process Step],
we [V2: Treatment]
using [V3: Capability]
to drive [V4: Benefit]
Example: At construction progress monitoring, we Automate reporting using Capture (drones) + Analyze (BIM comparison) to drive Visibility → which delivers Speed (faster issue resolution) and Quality (earlier defect detection).
Layer 03

Evaluation Layer

Once you've generated an intervention using the canvas, assess it on these six dimensions to prioritize and sequence.

DimensionWhat to assess
StakeholdersWho is involved at this stage? Who needs to change behavior? Internal teams, customers, contractors, regulators, investors.
FeasibilityFour sub-dimensions: Technical (can we build it?), Organizational (will people adopt?), Regulatory (is it allowed?), Financial (can we fund it?).
ImpactRevenue uplift, cost reduction, time saving, risk reduction, CX improvement — quantified where possible. Also: what specific current pain point does this solve?
Criticality TierFoundational — must-have to operate the entity at all. Competitive — needed to match best-in-class developers. Frontier — first-mover, category-creating.
DependenciesWhich foundational choices must be resolved first? Which other interventions are prerequisites? Critical for sequencing — some interventions unlock 10 others.
